# Really Bad Design Museum

A playful, interactive museum of thirty-five terrible website ideas. Built with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with no build step or runtime dependencies.

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 18 or later.

```sh
npm start
```

Open **http://localhost:3000**. Set `PORT` to use a different port.

The site deploys to Cloudflare Workers with `npm run build` followed by `npm run deploy`. The build creates `dist/` with the browser assets, 35 per-exhibit share pages, and 36 social-preview images. Clean URLs such as `/exhibit/cat-captcha` contain their own title, description, canonical URL, and 1200×630 Open Graph/Twitter artwork; the Worker injects the active workers.dev or custom-domain origin. Legacy hash exhibit URLs open normally and upgrade to their clean shareable route in the browser. Google Fonts is optional; local font fallbacks are included.

## The collection

- The CAT-PCHA (`#exhibit/cat-captcha`): prove you are human by being a mouse. Collect three cheeses in a maze and reach the mouse hole while a cat follows the shortest route toward you after every second move. Worse mode requires four cheeses and moves the cat every turn; both modes have escape routes. Getting caught restarts verification, not the whole museum. Arrow keys, WASD, direction buttons, and tapping adjacent squares all work. The chase is turn-based, and reduced motion removes movement transitions. Fixed mode replaces the game with a demo checkbox and confirmation. Neither mode performs a real security check.
- The Tetris Volume Control (`#exhibit/tetris-volume`): move, rotate, and drop tetrominoes across a 10-by-12 board. Every settled cell controls a pretend volume slider, with 80 cells—two-thirds of the board—representing 100%. Completed rows disappear and lower the volume with them. Another block replaces only the falling piece. Arrow keys move/lower blocks; Up or R rotates with either the board or a game button focused. Space drops on the board and activates focused buttons normally. The game pauses on demand, window blur, or tab hiding; reduced motion uses manual drops. Worse mode accelerates gravity. Empty speaker resets a jammed stack; fixed mode uses a normal slider. No audio plays and device volume never changes.
- The Seismic Text Editor (`#exhibit/seismic-editor`): every edit shakes the exhibit and increases the chance that the whole sentence tumbles into a pile of letter tiles. Slow typing produces gentle tremors, rapid edits amplify them, and newly entered punctuation adds a jolt, with extra force for exclamation marks. Pausing for 800 milliseconds returns the next edit to the gentle baseline. Pasting adds stress for each added character; reaching 100% stress guarantees a collapse. A collapse pauses editing but preserves the exact input, and Rebuild restores the letters and resets stress and typing cadence. Worse mode builds stress twice as fast and shakes harder. Fixed mode removes shaking and collapse risk. Reduced-motion preferences disable both animations, and the museum's controls never move.
- The Checkbox Ecosystem (`#exhibit/checkbox-ecosystem`): checked preferences need feeding before their health runs out and they uncheck themselves. Neglected cards wander inside the habitat. Configure three requested settings, keep the other boxes off, and save to stop the ecosystem. Worse mode decays faster and creates checked offspring every third feeding, up to twelve preferences. Pausing holds the habitat, reduced motion stops wandering, and fixed mode offers stable independent checkboxes. Only local demo preferences change.
- The Password Crane Game (`#exhibit/password-crane`): steer a claw across all 128 ASCII characters in 16 banks of eight hoppers, plus a drop chute. Assemble the visible fictional password `Claw_M00n!42`, or experiment with up to 80 characters. Control characters have named tiles and appear as tokens such as `[NUL]` and `[TAB]` in the output; each represents one actual character. Arrow keys steer, Page Up/Down change banks, Home/End jump to the first hopper/chute, and Space operates the claw with the cabinet focused. Held tiles survive bank changes. Worse mode grabs the next ASCII character every third attempt. Return tiles, undo, or reset to recover. Fixed mode uses a normal invented-phrase field. Never use real credentials.
- The Physics Shopping Cart (`#exhibit/physics-cart`): adding the first fictional product immediately starts a wheeled cart rolling downhill. More weight means more cartoon acceleration toward a pretend Buy now zone. Brake, pull back, pause, or return to the start without losing your basket. Pausing continues to hold the cart while you edit items. Worse mode adds a steeper slope and a speed bump. Reduced motion offers manual half-second steps instead of automatic rolling. Fixed mode uses a stationary basket and an explicit simulated checkout. There are no real purchases or payments.
- The Volume Seesaw (`#exhibit/volume-seesaw`): add pebbles, bricks, and anvils to either end of a wobbling beam. Its angle drives a silent volume slider; hold the setting to freeze it, or remove weights and rebalance. Worse mode adds buoyant balloons and rolls a weight to the opposite side every third addition. Reduced motion settles instantly, and leaving the tab pauses active wobbling. Fixed mode uses a normal slider. No audio plays or device volume changes.
- The Windswept Volume Slider (`#exhibit/wind-volume`): drag a vertical slider through gusts that twist its track and shove the simulated volume around. Pointer movement is projected onto the rotating track, so up stops reliably meaning louder. Letting go releases the handle, but the wind keeps moving the value until Save shelters it at 35-39% (the target is 37%). Worse mode brings stronger gusts and possible upside-down controls. Keyboard controls work too; reduced motion freezes the track but retains numeric wind interference. Fixed mode provides a stable, predictable slider. No audio plays, no audio APIs are used, and device volume never changes.
- The Notification Fly Swatter (`#exhibit/notification-swatter`): the first keystroke or paste automatically starts in-page alerts. Complete six fields (name, email, organization, subject, message, and the code QUIET), then swat the remaining notifications. Pause continues to hold the swarm while editing. Alerts cover the scrollable form but never the outside Pause and Swat one controls. Worse mode spawns faster and creates alerts for misses. Keyboard, touch, and reduced-motion alternatives are available; completion or leaving the exhibit stops the swarm. Fixed mode has no alerts. No browser notification permissions are requested.
- The Email Address Auction (`#exhibit/email-auction`): opens directly onto a catalog of all 94 non-space printable ASCII characters, without requesting an email first. Browse/filter lowercase letters, uppercase letters, digits, and punctuation; bid for 1-20 copies at a time and rebuy characters as needed. Vowels attract competition and `@` has a reserve price. Won copies go into a tray: assemble any valid email up to 254 characters, return mistakes, or drag characters to reorder them. Only winning bids spend imaginary coins. Worse mode raises prices and competition. Restart a lot without losing winnings, or reset the full auction. Fixed mode uses a normal email field. No storage, email, money, or external requests are involved.
- The Elevator Date Picker (`#exhibit/elevator-date`): ride between year floors, then transfer to separate month and day elevators. Request a stop to reach the next numbered floor before selecting it. Worse mode skips three floors per departure unless a stop is requested. Dates from 1900 through 2100 are supported, including correct month lengths and leap years. Leaving the tab pauses travel; reset returns to the year lobby. Fixed mode uses a normal date field. Nothing is booked.
- The Expanding Form (`#exhibit/expanding-form`): a conventional contact form with name, email, subject, and message fields. Extra spacing grows exponentially as `32 * (2^(characters/5) - 1)` pixels per field, capped at 60,000 pixels. The final Message field sends the submit button even farther away: `128 * (2^(characters/3) - 1)`, capped at 360,000 pixels. Worse mode reduces the exponent scales to three and two characters respectively, triples both caps, and adds folding effects. Compress temporarily removes gaps without losing answers; typing expands them again. The viewport stays bounded, reduced motion removes folding/animation, and fixed mode keeps stable spacing.
- The Self-Correcting Search Bar (`#exhibit/correcting-search`): paused typing replaces one word with a close spelling from a compact bundled vocabulary. Matching uses edit distance with adjacent transpositions, a maximum distance of one for short words or two for longer ones, and preserves case, punctuation, and surrounding text. Reject up to three suggestions (five plus short explanations in worse mode) to search the original query. Unmatched words stay unchanged and remain searchable. There is no large dictionary payload, download, or external search service. Fixed mode searches the entered query directly.
- The Shrinking Unsubscribe Button (`#exhibit/shrinking-unsubscribe`): starts with an explicitly fictional subscription. Approaching the cancellation button with a mouse makes it shrink until it relocates to a distant position and reappears at full size. Worse mode shrinks sooner and relocates earlier. Keyboard, touch, reduced-motion settings, and fixed mode provide a stable cancellation path. Cancellation changes only the local demo state; no real account, subscription, or payment exists.
- The Phone Number Casino: roll and lock ten digits to enter the fictional number 2025550107. Worse mode also rerolls and unlocks the digit to the left, rewarding a right-to-left strategy.
- The Password Gym (`#exhibit/password-gym`): satisfy 20 progressively revealed rules, including a digit sum, weather reference, apology, color, and year. Rules advance automatically as you type or paste, with all earlier requirements still enforced and no submit clicks. The default is solvable. Worse mode expands to 32 rules and is deliberately impossible: its final rule forbids digits while earlier rules require them. The contradiction is explained when reached. Fixed mode checks one visible length requirement automatically. **Never enter a real password or reuse a puzzle solution for an account.** This is not a strength evaluator.
- The Dropdown Word Processor (`#exhibit/word-editor`): compose a document using a separate dropdown for every character, including spaces, punctuation, and line breaks. Reopen a dropdown to replace a letter, delete selected characters, and undo or redo text edits. Worse mode reshuffles each menu after every edit; fixed mode restores normal typing. Replaces the former Everything Is Urgent exhibit.
- The Cookie Switchboard: reject four optional cookie categories with coupled switches. Worse mode flips three switches per click and negates their labels. All modes are solvable, and no actual cookies are used.
- The Address Jigsaw (`#exhibit/address-jigsaw`): assemble `42 Waffle Lane, Apt 7B, Cloud City, CA 90210` from nine shuffled pieces. Tap or keyboard-activate pieces to fill slots; return pieces by activating filled slots, or drag pieces to replace/swap specific slots. Worse mode includes four decoys and reshuffles the tray after edits. Fixed mode allows typing/pasting the fictional address. Nothing is shipped.
- The Runaway Button: flees to randomized, distant positions across an arena. Worse mode detects approaching cursors, shrinks the target, and leaves clickable decoys.
- The Character Bureaucracy (`#exhibit/dropdown`): tune a 0–9999 Hz slider to an exact character frequency, lock it, and collect three ordered approval stamps before appending one character. The alphabet reshuffles after every character. Worse mode requires five stamps, changes frequency offsets, destroys incorrect permits, and makes Undo delete two characters.
- Terms & Conditions: The Game (`#exhibit/terms-game`): an 80-clause fictional agreement must be scrolled to the end before an eight-question, open-book reading exam unlocks. Answers are buried in the document and must be correct before acceptance becomes available. Worse mode doubles the document to 160 clauses, asks twelve questions, and resets the exam after a wrong answer. Declining is always available. Fixed mode offers a short summary with immediate accept/decline choices. No real agreement is created.
- The Retro Personal Homepage: navigation goes to the wrong section, nickname input reverses after every edit, and signing the guestbook requires a cat CAPTCHA. Worse mode replaces vowels, rearranges CAPTCHA tiles after each selection, and demands two rounds.
- The AI Everything Store (`#exhibit/ai-store`): prompt-engineer a spoon, umbrella, or rock before activating its fictional monthly plan. Worse mode requires three calibration rounds. Fixed mode sells ordinary objects at one-time prices. Responses are scripted locally, not generated by an AI service.
- The Font Buffet (`#exhibit/fonts`): edit a sentence whose words receive random fonts, sizes, colors, and rotations. Worse mode styles each character separately. "Make fonts agree" remixes them instead; fixed mode uses consistent typography.
- The Mystery Meat Menu (`#exhibit/mystery-menu`): find and acknowledge a shipping policy, then retrieve a fictional receipt through six unlabeled symbols with useless tooltips. Worse mode reshuffles the icon mappings after every navigation click. Fixed mode supplies stable, descriptive labels.
- The Unix Birthday Picker (`#exhibit/unix-birthday`): slide through Unix timestamps in **milliseconds**, with a live UTC date/time preview and one-hour keyboard steps. A small Align to UTC midnight button snaps to the start of the selected UTC date before confirmation. Dates from January 1, 1900 through today are supported, including negative timestamps. Worse mode is a single manual timestamp field with live UTC date/time conversion and automatic birthday validation, without retyping, timezone selection, a confirmation button, or an alignment helper. The preview also converts representable timestamps outside the birthday range while validation explains why they cannot be accepted. Fixed mode uses a native date field.
- The Alphabet Shuffle (`#exhibit/alphabet`): a separate character-entry slider with a randomly ordered alphabet that reshuffles after every appended character. Worse mode also reshuffles on committed slider adjustments. The visible preview always shows the character that will be appended.
- The Horizontal Lifestyle (`#exhibit/horizontal`): navigate a four-panel sideways website to reach a demo contact form. The horizontal scrolling stays inside the exhibit. Section buttons, keyboard arrows, touch gestures, and the mouse wheel work; worse mode reverses the arrow buttons and vertical wheel direction. Fixed mode restores vertical page flow.
- The Cancellation Labyrinth: navigate four double-negative confirmations to cancel a fictional subscription. Worse mode adds two checkpoints, shuffles button positions, and resets progress on a wrong answer.
- The Recipe Odyssey: six mandatory reading-comprehension quizzes stand between you and toast. The fake skip button detours to an ad. Worse mode resets reading progress after wrong answers or attempts to skip.
- The Corporate Fog Machine: mandatory onboarding adds more steps every time you complete one. Going back discards your progress.
- The Loading Experience (`#exhibit/loading`): ten fake progress stages, including a backwards jump from 99%, reveal one sentence. Worse mode pauses for three approvals. Loading can be cancelled, and timers stop on reset or navigation. Fixed mode reveals the sentence immediately.
- The Volume Gym: reach exactly 37% using +7/-3 buttons with wraparound at 0 and 100. Worse mode uses +17/-11 and loses one point every two seconds after the first adjustment. Success stops the decay. No audio is played.
- The Calendar Treadmill: navigate from January 1 to January 12, 2000, using one-day buttons. Worse mode makes Forward alternate between advancing seven days and retreating six.

Every exhibit has original, worse, and fixed modes, a reset button, a curator's note, and a persistent mode toolbar. Use **Escape** to return to the collection. Filter the collection by category or navigate directly to an exhibit, such as `#exhibit/runaway`.

Interactions are local and ephemeral. Messages, guestbook entries, and purchases are simulations; no data is submitted or stored. These are deliberately frustrating task interfaces, not just visual parodies. The museum's sticky Exit, Reset, and Fix it controls remain trustworthy. Fixed mode removes the obstacles rather than merely restyling them.

There are no flashing effects or browser traps. The runaway button remains usable by keyboard and touch and respects reduced-motion preferences. All other tasks can also be operated with a keyboard. The character dial supports arrow keys and one-unit adjustment buttons.
